02 — Password & Account Security

Passwords protect your important online accounts. Safe password habits help keep email, banking, social media, work, and school accounts more secure.

Welcome

Many everyday activities depend on accounts: email, banking, social media, education, work systems, and online shopping. Passwords are one of the most important ways to protect those accounts.

Strong, thoughtful password habits reduce the chance of unauthorized access and help keep personal information safer.

Why Passwords Matter

A password is one layer of account protection. If it is weak, guessed, or reused, an attacker may gain access to more than one account.

🔒

One layer of protection

Passwords help protect accounts, but they work best when combined with good habits and additional safeguards such as MFA.

⚠️

Weak passwords increase risk

Short or common passwords are easier to guess or brute-force. This can make personal data and online accounts more vulnerable.

🔁

Password reuse creates danger

If one account is exposed, attackers may try the same password elsewhere. This can lead to multiple accounts being compromised.

Strong Passwords

A strong password is difficult to guess and not easy to predict. It should be specific to the account and should not rely on personal information.

  • Use long passwords or passphrases.
  • Use a different password for important accounts.
  • Avoid obvious information such as names, birthdays, pet names, or common phrases.
  • Avoid common patterns or very short passwords.
  • Consider using a reputable password manager to help manage unique passwords.

Password Reuse

Reusing the same password across multiple websites increases risk. If one website is compromised, the same password may be tested on other accounts.

Example

One website compromised → Password exposed → Same password used elsewhere → Multiple accounts at risk

Using unique passwords limits the damage. Even if one account is exposed, the others remain better protected.

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

MFA adds another layer of protection beyond a password. Instead of relying on only one factor, the user must confirm their identity in another way.

Something you know

Password

A secret you remember, such as a password or passphrase.

This is the first factor, but it can be stolen or guessed.

Something you have

Authenticator or device

A phone, security key, or code generator used to confirm access.

This adds a second factor and makes unauthorized access harder.

Something you are

Biometric check

Such as a fingerprint or facial scan used on trusted devices.

Biometric checks can strengthen login security when supported by the device or service.

Account Protection

These habits help reduce everyday account risk:

  • Enable MFA when it is available.
  • Use unique passwords for important accounts.
  • Keep recovery information updated.
  • Review account security settings when you can.
  • Be careful with login alerts and notifications.
  • Do not share passwords or authentication codes.
  • Do not approve unexpected authentication requests.
  • Sign out of accounts on shared or public devices.
  • Keep devices and browsers updated.

Key Takeaways

  • Use long, unique passwords or passphrases.
  • Avoid password reuse.
  • Consider a password manager.
  • Enable MFA whenever possible.
  • Never share passwords or authentication codes.
  • Be cautious with unexpected login or security messages.
  • Verify suspicious requests through trusted channels.
