Welcome

In everyday life, we use phones, computers, email, messaging, banking apps, and work or school accounts. Cybersecurity helps us protect those tools and the information we rely on every day.

Good cybersecurity habits help prevent unauthorized access, scams, data loss, and disruption. It is not only a technical topic. It is a practical skill for everyone.

What is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity means protecting digital systems, data, and activities from harm. It includes keeping devices, accounts, and information safe from unauthorized access, mistakes, and malicious actions.

🛡️

What we protect

We protect phones, computers, networks, passwords, messages, documents, photos, online accounts, and the services people rely on each day.

👥

Who can be affected

Anyone can be affected: students, workers, families, business owners, and organizations. Cybersecurity is relevant for everyday life.

💡

Why it is not only an IT issue

People make security decisions every day — such as choosing a password, checking a link, sharing information, or avoiding suspicious messages. These decisions matter.

Everyday examples include protecting your email, safeguarding a social media account, being careful with online banking, and using safer habits at work or school.

Why Cybersecurity Matters

Poor cybersecurity habits can lead to serious problems for both individuals and organizations. When systems and accounts are not protected, the consequences may be stressful and expensive.

  • Stolen accounts: attackers may access email, social media, or banking accounts.
  • Identity misuse: personal information can be used without permission.
  • Financial loss: money may be stolen through scams or fake transactions.
  • Privacy loss: private photos, messages, and documents may be exposed.
  • Data loss: important files may be deleted, hidden, or locked.
  • Service disruption: essential work, education, or communication may be interrupted.
  • Damage to organizations: businesses and institutions can lose trust, time, and resources.

CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is a simple model used to explain the main goals of cybersecurity. It helps us think about protecting information and systems.

Confidentiality

Confidentiality

Definition: Only authorized people should access information.

Everyday example: A private password or personal document should not be exposed to unauthorized people.

Why it matters: Protecting confidentiality helps prevent spying, unauthorized access, and information leaks.

Integrity

Integrity

Definition: Information should remain accurate, complete, and trustworthy.

Everyday example: An attacker should not secretly change important information, such as account details or messages.

Why it matters: We need to know that the information we rely on has not been changed or tampered with.

Availability

Availability

Definition: Systems and information should be available when authorized people need them.

Everyday example: A service should remain accessible when users need it, such as a school portal, email, or a banking app.

Why it matters: If systems are unavailable, people cannot complete essential tasks or access important information.

Common Cybersecurity Threats

Awareness is the first step toward protection. These are common examples of risks people may face online and in digital communication.

Phishing

What it is: A fake message or website that looks trustworthy.

What it can cause: Stolen login information or fake requests for personal data.

Warning sign: It creates urgency or asks for immediate action.

Safe action: Verify the sender or website before clicking or entering information.

Malware

What it is: Harmful software designed to harm devices or steal information.

What it can cause: Slower devices, stolen files, or hidden access.

Warning sign: Strange pop-ups, unexpected downloads, or unusual performance.

Safe action: Only install software from trusted sources and keep systems updated.

Weak or Reused Passwords

What it is: Passwords that are easy to guess or used across many accounts.

What it can cause: Account takeover and loss of access.

Warning sign: Known words, short passwords, or the same password everywhere.

Safe action: Use unique, strong passwords and a trusted password manager if available.

Social Engineering

What it is: Manipulation designed to trick people into sharing information or taking action.

What it can cause: False trust, unsafe decisions, and information disclosure.

Warning sign: Pressure, fear, or requests to act quickly.

Safe action: Pause, verify the request, and confirm through a trusted channel.

Unsafe Websites

What it is: Websites that mimic trusted services or ask for unsafe actions.

What it can cause: Identity theft, fake login attempts, or download of harmful content.

Warning sign: Spelling mistakes, odd URLs, or missing security indicators.

Safe action: Check the address carefully and do not enter personal data on unknown sites.

Account Takeover

What it is: Someone gains access to a person’s account without permission.

What it can cause: Abuse of messages, financial loss, or privacy breaches.

Warning sign: Unexpected password reset messages or unrecognized activity.

Safe action: Change passwords quickly and enable stronger sign-in protections.

Data Exposure

What it is: Sensitive information is shared or exposed without proper protection.

What it can cause: Privacy concerns, misuse of personal details, and loss of trust.

Warning sign: Oversharing on public platforms or sending personal information through insecure channels.

Safe action: Share only what is necessary and use protected communication methods.

Key Takeaways

  • Cybersecurity protects people, information, devices, and services.
  • Everyone has a role in cybersecurity.
  • Confidentiality protects information from unauthorized access.
  • Integrity protects information from unauthorized changes.
  • Availability keeps systems and information usable.
