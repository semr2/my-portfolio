06 — Data Protection & Privacy

Protecting personal and sensitive information is a core part of cybersecurity. Good data-handling habits help prevent exposure, loss, and misuse of information in everyday work and digital life.

Welcome

Data protection means taking care of information so it is not lost, exposed, shared too widely, or used in ways that are not appropriate. Privacy is not only about legal rules — it is also about everyday decisions that reduce risk.

Ordinary users are important to data security. A careful habit like verifying recipients, locking devices, or checking sharing permissions can prevent a serious problem before it happens.

Core idea

If information is sensitive, private, or not needed for the task, it should be protected and shared carefully.

What Is Personal Data?

Personal data includes information that helps identify a person or can be used to learn more about them. Some information may seem harmless on its own, but combined with other details it can become sensitive.

👤

Basic personal details

Full name, phone number, email address, address information, and account usernames.

🆔

Identifiers

ID numbers, employee or student records, official document numbers, and unique online identifiers.

📍

Location and activity

Home or workplace information, device activity, geolocation data, and photos or videos that reveal identity or routines.

Sensitive Information

Sensitive information is information that would cause harm if exposed, misused, or shared without permission. Some information is more sensitive because it can enable fraud, compromise an account, or expose private details.

  • Passwords and authentication codes
  • Financial information
  • Identification documents
  • Health or medical details
  • Confidential company or school information
  • Private communications, personal records, and access credentials

Rule of thumb: if you would not want the information publicly exposed, treat it carefully.

Data Minimization

Data minimization means collecting, using, storing, and sharing only the information that is needed for the task. Unnecessary data increases risk and makes mistakes more damaging.

Good principle: only collect and share what is actually necessary.

Examples

Bad: asking for unnecessary personal information. Better: requesting only the information required for the task.

Safe Data Sharing

Every time you share data, pause and check whether it is necessary, whether the receiver is correct, and whether the channel is appropriate. Good sharing habits reduce accidental exposure.

  • Who is receiving the information?
  • Why do they need it?
  • Is the communication channel appropriate?
  • Is the recipient correct?
  • Are there correct permissions and access controls?
  • Is the information more detailed than necessary?

Protecting Files and Documents

Sensitive files should be kept in approved locations, only accessed by authorized people, and shared only when needed. Protecting documents is not just about making a file strong; it is also about controlling who can access it and how it is stored.

  • Use appropriate access controls.
  • Avoid unnecessary sharing.
  • Lock devices when unattended.
  • Protect files with strong access methods.
  • Keep sensitive documents in approved storage.
  • Remove unnecessary copies and dispose of files securely.

Privacy on Websites and Applications

Applications and websites often request personal information, access to files, or unnecessary permissions. Before providing information online, ask: why is it needed, is this the correct website, and what data is being shared?

  • Check permissions and app access requests.
  • Be careful with forms that ask for more information than needed.
  • Use trusted websites and verify the address.
  • Review privacy settings and public profile information.
  • Reduce unnecessary personal information shared across services.

Before providing personal information online, stop and ask why it is needed.

Key Takeaways

  • Personal data has value and should be protected.
  • Sensitive information requires extra care.
  • Only collect and share the information needed for the task.
  • Verify the correct recipient before sending data.
  • Use appropriate storage, permissions, and access controls.
  • Never share passwords or authentication codes.
  • Report mistakes or suspected exposure quickly.
  • Privacy is an essential part of cybersecurity.
